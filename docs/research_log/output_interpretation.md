# Output Interpretation

---

### What Does the Output Mean for 1 Stream? (Vlasov-Poisson Code Explanation)

**What is a "stream"?**

A "stream" is a distinct group of particles in phase space, think of it as a separate population of plasma with its own charge density and velocity profile.

---

### Key Variables

- **\(q\):** Charge density (scalar field on the mesh)
- **\(u\):** Velocity (vector field on the mesh)
- **\(\phi\):** Electrostatic potential (scalar field on the mesh)

---

### Code Output

Every 20 steps, the code writes out:

- The charge density \(q\)
- The electrostatic potential \(\phi\)
- The velocity field \(u\)

These are saved to the file:

`advection_multiple_streams.pvd` (which can be visualized in ParaView or similar software).

---

### Physical Interpretation (for 1 stream):

1. **Charge Density (\(q\)):**
    - Shows how the charge is distributed in space.
    - Initially a Gaussian bump (a localized "blob" of charge).
    - Over time, this blob moves and deforms under the influence of the electric field generated by itself (through \(\phi\)).
2. **Electrostatic Potential (\(\phi\)):**
    - Solved from the Poisson equation using the current charge density.
    - Represents the potential landscape created by the charge.
    - Its slope gives the electric field, which influences the velocity field.
3. **Velocity Field (\(u\)):**
    - Represents the average velocity of the stream at each point in space.
    - Initially set by a sinusoidal profile.
    - Evolves in time as the electric field accelerates or decelerates the charge.

---

### What to Expect in the Output

- **Plotting q over space and time:**
The initial Gaussian "blob" of charge will move, and may stretch or compress depending on the evolving electric field.
- **Plotting \(\phi\):**
You'll see a potential well or hill that follows the charge distribution.
- **Plotting \(u\):**
The velocity profile will change in response to the electric field generated by the charge.

---

**Summary:**

For a single stream, you are simulating the evolution of a single "blob" of charge as it moves and interacts with itself through the self-consistent electric field. The output files let you visualize how the charge density, velocity, and potential change with time.
